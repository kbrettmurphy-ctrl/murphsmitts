# MurphOS Copywriting Guidelines

**Version:** 1.1
**Status:** Draft
**Last Updated:** June 2026

---

# Why This Exists

Murph's Mitts communicates through more than design.

Every headline, button, form label, email, text message, error state, and service description shapes how customers feel about the business.

Copy should reduce friction, build trust, and reflect the same care that goes into every glove.

The goal is simple:

Say what needs to be said.

Say it clearly.

Say it honestly.

Then stop.

---

# Copywriting Principles

## Clarity First

Customers should never have to decode what we mean.

Use plain language.

Use correct glove terminology.

Avoid cleverness when clarity matters.

A customer should always understand:

* What is being offered.
* What happened.
* What happens next.
* What is expected of them.
* What they can expect from Murph's Mitts.

---

## Honest Over Impressive

Do not overstate what Murph's Mitts does.

Do not promise museum-quality restoration, leather repair, repainting, rebuilding, or miracle work unless that service is truly offered.

Murph's Mitts brings gloves back to life through cleaning, conditioning, relacing, reshaping, and thoughtful care.

The work should sound skilled without sounding exaggerated.

Confidence does not require hype.

---

## Care Without Judgment

Many customers treat their gloves like tools because that is what gloves are on the field.

Murph's Mitts treats them like something worth caring for.

Copy should never shame customers for dirty gloves, neglected leather, broken lace, hot cars, bat bags, zip ties, 550 cord, or whatever field-expedient nonsense humanity has managed to invent this week.

Educate.

Do not scold.

Guide.

Do not guilt.

---

## Preserve Character

A revived glove should not be described as brand new.

Used gloves have patina, wear, memory, and character.

Murph's Mitts does not erase that history.

Copy should make this clear:

The goal is not to make every glove look new.

The goal is to make the glove feel alive, cared for, structured, and ready for more seasons.

---

## Fewer Words, More Meaning

Premium brands do not explain everything at once.

Use short sections.

Use clean headings.

Keep paragraphs tight.

Avoid long, crowded blocks of text.

If a sentence does not reduce friction, build trust, or showcase craftsmanship, remove it.

---

# Brand Voice

Murph's Mitts sounds like an experienced craftsman.

Clear.

Calm.

Direct.

Honest.

Confident.

Human.

The voice should feel personal without becoming casual to the point of sounding careless.

Murph's Mitts should sound like a person who knows what he is doing and respects the customer enough not to waste their time.

---

## Small Business Tone

Murph's Mitts should sound polished, but never corporate.

The customer should feel like they are dealing directly with Brett, not a faceless repair company, national repair chain, or automated service desk.

The tone should reflect a one-man shop that is organized, professional, and personally invested in the work.

Emails and messages may be automated, but they should still feel human.

The customer should feel like there is a real person on the other end who knows the glove, understands the order, and is paying attention.

Murph's Mitts should carry the tone of a trusted local business.

The kind of business where people in Surf City, Hampstead, and the surrounding area know who is doing the work.

Professional does not have to mean cold.

Personal does not have to mean sloppy.

Murph's Mitts should live in the middle:

Small-business personal.

Craftsman-level careful.

Modern enough to feel organized.

Human enough to feel trusted.

Avoid language that feels cold, generic, overly formal, mass-produced, or like it escaped from a corporate customer-service portal and is now loose in society.

# Service Language

Use service language that is accurate and honest.

## Cleaning

Cleaning removes dirt, sweat, grime, and buildup from the glove.

Do not imply that cleaning will erase all discoloration, wear, stains, or age.

---

## Conditioning

Conditioning helps restore moisture, feel, and flexibility to dry leather.

Do not imply that conditioning reverses all leather damage.

---

## Relacing

Relacing replaces worn, broken, stretched, dry, cheap, or failing lace with quality leather lace.

Relacing helps restore structure, tension, shape, and confidence.

---

## Revival

Revival is the overall experience of bringing a glove back to life.

It may include cleaning, conditioning, relacing, reshaping, and care.

Revival does not mean making the glove look new.

It means returning the glove as the best version of its current self.

---

# Customer Expectations

Copy should set clear expectations.

Be honest about what Murph's Mitts can and cannot do.

## Good Expectation

A cleaned and conditioned glove may still show age, staining, discoloration, scars, creases, and patina.

Those marks are part of the glove's story.

---

## Bad Expectation

Your glove will look brand new.

---

## Good Expectation

A full relace can restore structure and help your glove feel secure again.

---

## Bad Expectation

A full relace will fix every issue with the glove.

---

# Headlines

Headlines should be simple and confident.

Avoid hype.

Avoid shouting.

Avoid clever lines that make the customer think too hard.

## Good Headlines

* Bring Your Glove Back to Life
* Professional Glove Relacing & Care
* Built for More Seasons
* Cleaned. Conditioned. Relaced.
* Respect the Glove's Story
* Ready for the Next Game

## Avoid Headlines

* Best Glove Repair in the World
* We Make Gloves Brand New
* Ultimate Pro-Level Elite Restoration
* Your Glove Will Never Be the Same
* Premium Dynamic Leather Solutions

If a headline sounds like it was written by a marketing department trapped in a conference room, delete it.

---

# Calls to Action

Calls to action should be clear and calm.

They should guide the customer, not pressure them.

## Preferred CTAs

* Start a Service Request
* Request Service
* View Services
* See the Process
* View Gallery
* Contact Murph's Mitts
* Check Availability
* Learn More

---

## Avoid CTAs

* Buy Now
* Order Now!!!
* Get Yours Today
* Don't Miss Out
* Transform My Glove
* Make It New

The customer is trusting Murph's Mitts with something personal.

Do not make the experience feel like a flash sale.

# Forms

Form copy should feel helpful and human.

## Form Label Rules

* Use clear labels.
* Keep helper text short.
* Explain only when explanation helps.
* Use correct glove terms.
* Avoid placeholder-only instructions.
* Avoid unnecessary jokes in required fields or error states.

## Good Form Copy

**Primary Lace Color**
Choose the main lace color for your glove.

**Custom Color Request**
Don't see the color you want? Describe it here.

**Glove Notes**
Tell me anything I should know about the glove, its condition, or what you want done.

# Transactional Communication

Transactional communication includes emails, text messages, form confirmations, status updates, order updates, estimate messages, payment instructions, shipping instructions, pickup coordination, and completion messages.

These messages should reduce uncertainty.

Customers should never have to wonder what happens next after they submit a request, approve an estimate, ship a glove, receive an update, or complete an order.

Every transactional message should answer:

* What happened?
* What is the current status?
* Is action needed?
* What happens next?

If the customer needs to take action, say so clearly.

If no action is needed, say that too.

A good message prevents a follow-up question.

A great message makes the customer feel like the process is already handled.

---

# Emails

Email copy should be warm, direct, and useful.

Every email should answer:

* What happened?
* What is the current status?
* Is action needed?
* What happens next?

## Email Style

* Use the customer's first name when available.
* Keep paragraphs short.
* Avoid robotic status language.
* Include order number when helpful.
* Use clear next steps.
* End with a simple sign-off.
* Sound like Brett, not a software notification wearing a fake mustache.

## Email Tone

Helpful.

Calm.

Personal.

Professional.

Not overly polished.

Not overly casual.

Not corporate.

Not generic.

Emails should feel like they came from a real one-man shop that has its process together.

---

## Estimate Sent Emails

Estimate emails should clearly explain the quote, what work is included, and what the customer needs to do next.

The message should be brief and clear.

The customer should understand whether they need to approve, decline, ask a question, ship the glove, schedule drop-off, or wait for the next update.

---

## Estimate Approval Emails

When a customer approves an estimate, Murph's Mitts should send a short next-step message based on how the glove is getting to Murph's Mitts.

For shipped orders, the message should include clear shipping instructions.

For local orders, the message should explain that drop-off or pickup coordination will happen next.

This email should be brief, practical, and reassuring.

The customer should not have to ask:

"How do I get the glove to you?"

That answer should already be waiting for them.

---

## Shipping Instruction Emails

Shipping instruction emails should be simple and practical.

They should tell the customer:

* Where to send the glove.
* How to package it.
* Whether to include anything in the box.
* Whether tracking is recommended.
* What happens after the glove arrives.

Shipping instructions should avoid unnecessary detail, but they should answer the obvious questions before the customer has to ask.

The tone should be helpful and personal.

Not warehouse.

Not corporate.

Not "Dear Valued Customer," because apparently businesses still say that like the customer is a coupon with legs.

---

## Local Drop-Off Emails

Local drop-off emails should make the next step clear without overcomplicating the process.

They should tell the customer:

* That the estimate was approved.
* That drop-off coordination is next.
* Whether Murph's Mitts will follow up.
* Whether the customer needs to reply.
* Any basic timing expectations.

Local customers should feel like they are dealing with someone nearby, not entering a national ticketing system.

---

## Status Update Emails

Status update emails should be short and useful.

They should tell the customer what changed and what happens next.

Avoid long explanations unless the status truly requires it.

## Completion Emails

Completion emails should clearly explain that the glove is finished and ready for pickup, delivery, or shipping.

If payment is still needed, payment instructions should be clear and easy to follow.

If shipping is involved, tracking information should be included when available.

---

# SMS

SMS copy should be short.

Text messages should only include what the customer needs right now.

## SMS Rules

* Keep it direct.
* Avoid long explanations.
* Use plain language.
* Include action steps only when needed.
* Do not use marketing language.
* Do not overload the customer.
* Do not send a text if an email is the better place for the details.

## Good SMS Style

Your estimate is ready. Reply YES to approve or NO to place the request on hold.

Your glove is finished and ready for pickup. I’ll follow up to coordinate a time.

Work has started on your glove. Estimated completion: June 30.

---

## SMS vs Email

Use SMS for quick updates and simple actions.

Use email for longer details, instructions, estimates, payment links, shipping instructions, or anything the customer may need to reference later.

SMS should point.

Email should explain.

---

# Error Messages

Error messages should be calm, specific, and useful.

Do not blame the customer.

Do not use technical language unless the user is an admin and the detail helps solve the problem.

## Good Error Copy

Please enter your email address.

Please choose a primary lace color.

Something went wrong while submitting your request. Your information was not sent. Please try again.

## Bad Error Copy

Invalid input.

Submission failed.

Error 400.

Required field missing.

---

# Empty States

Empty states should explain what is missing and what the user can do next.

## Good Empty State Copy

No gloves are available right now.

No matching orders found.

No photos have been uploaded yet.

No lace colors are currently marked as low.

## Bad Empty State Copy

Nothing here.

Empty.

No data.

---

# Admin Copy

Admin copy should prioritize speed and clarity.

The admin portal is a work tool.

It should not sound overly branded.

## Admin Copy Rules

* Use short labels.
* Use consistent status names.
* Avoid unnecessary explanatory text.
* Make actions obvious.
* Use confirmation messages only when helpful.
* Keep destructive actions clear.

## Good Admin Labels

* Save
* Delete Order
* View Order
* Send Email
* Mark Paid
* Ready to Go
* Waiting on Lace/Parts

---

# Personality

Murph's Mitts may have personality.

It should not become a comedy routine.

Personality works best in:

* About page copy.
* Small supporting notes.
* Social captions.
* Occasional brand moments.
* Local/community references.
* Personal explanations from Brett.

Personality should be avoided in:

* Error messages.
* Payment instructions.
* Legal pages.
* Critical status updates.
* Required form guidance.
* Anything involving customer confusion or delay.

A little warmth builds trust.

Too much cleverness adds friction.

---

# Local Voice

Murph's Mitts is a small business with a real local presence.

The copy may reflect that.

Use local tone when it feels natural, especially around:

* Local drop-off.
* Pickup coordination.
* About page copy.
* Community references.
* Surf City and Hampstead area customers.
* Social content.
* Personal notes from Brett.

Do not overdo it.

The site should not sound like it is trying too hard to prove it is local.

Local trust comes from being clear, helpful, familiar, and consistent.

Not from stuffing every sentence with hometown references like a chamber of commerce brochure that learned to type.

# Do / Don't Examples

## Do

Say:

Murph's Mitts brings gloves back to life through cleaning, conditioning, relacing, and thoughtful care.

Do not say:

Murph's Mitts fully restores any glove to factory-new condition.

---

## Do

Say:

A full relace can restore structure and help your glove feel secure again.

Do not say:

A full relace will make your glove perfect.

---

## Do

Say:

Patina is part of the glove's story.

Do not say:

All marks and wear will be removed.

---

## Do

Say:

Submit a service request and I'll review your glove before confirming the final price.

Do not say:

Order now before spots fill up.

---

## Do

Say:

Your estimate is approved. Since this is a shipped order, I’ll send simple shipping instructions so you know exactly how to get the glove to me.

Do not say:

Congratulations! Your glove transformation journey has begun.

---

## Do

Say:

Once the glove arrives, I’ll check it in and send you an update.

Do not say:

Your item will be processed upon receipt.

---

# Writing Checklist

Before publishing copy, ask:

1. Is it clear?
2. Is it honest?
3. Is it useful?
4. Does it reduce friction?
5. Does it sound like Murph's Mitts?
6. Does it avoid overpromising?
7. Does it use correct glove terminology?
8. Does it answer what happens next?
9. Can it be shorter?
10. Can it be calmer?

If the answer to most of these is no, rewrite it.
